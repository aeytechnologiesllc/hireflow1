A good customer service agent for an online store turns "Where is my order?" into a calm customer who orders again. A careless one promises delivery dates nobody can keep, refunds outside your rules, or sends a parcel to the wrong person. Hire for the first. Test hard for the second.

This guide is for an agent who answers your customers by chat, email or both.

## What the job is

Most of the work follows the order, from before it is placed to after it arrives.

- **Before they buy.** Sizes, materials, what fits with what, how long shipping takes.
- **Where is my order.** Reading the tracking, explaining it plainly, and knowing when to contact the carrier.
- **Changes.** A new address, a different size, a cancel before it ships.
- **Problems.** Wrong, damaged or missing items.
- **Returns and exchanges.** Checking them against your rules and starting them.
- **Discount codes.** The code that did not work, or the sale that ended yesterday.
- **Passing things on.** Getting each problem to the right person: the warehouse, payments, or you.

On a good day, customers know where their parcels are, problems are fixed in one or two replies, and nothing is promised that the store cannot do.

## What they must never do

These rules matter more than speed or charm. Put them in the job post, in training and in your tests.

### Never promise a delivery date the carrier has not given

"It will be there Friday" feels kind. If the tracking does not say Friday, it is a guess, and the customer plans around it. A good agent gives the date the carrier shows and says what they will do if it slips.

### Never refund, replace or discount outside the rules

Write your rules down with the numbers in them: how many days for returns, who pays return shipping, and what the agent can offer alone. For example, a replacement for a damaged item once the customer sends a photo. Everything else goes to you or a named person.

### Never take card details by chat or email

Payments should go through your checkout or your payment provider's own page, never through a chat window or an inbox. If a customer types a card number into a chat anyway, the agent should not use it, should ask them not to send it again, and should follow your rule for removing it if your tools allow that.

### Never share an order with the wrong person

"My wife placed the order. What address is it going to?" The agent checks who they are talking to, using your rules, before sharing anything. A common check is the order number and the email address on the order.

### Never change an address without checking

A request to send an order somewhere new, from an email that is not on the order, can be a way to take someone else's parcel. Write down what the agent must check first.

## What to test

Test before you interview, using your own products and rules. Give everyone the same tests and the same short policy sheet.

1. **Tracking that stopped moving.** "My order hasn't moved in six days. Where is it?" A strong reply explains what the tracking shows in plain words, says what the agent will do next, and gives no date the carrier has not given.
2. **The wrong size, with a deadline.** "You sent a medium, I ordered a large, and I need it for Saturday." Look for a quick apology, the fix your rules allow, and honesty about whether Saturday is possible.
3. **The code that did not work.** "Your sale ended last night and my code didn't work at checkout. Can you still give me the discount?" Look for a kind, clear answer that follows your rule, whatever it is.
4. **The new address from a new email.** "Please send order 5521 to this address instead." It comes from an email that is not on the order. A strong reply makes no change until the check is done, and says why without accusing anyone.
5. **The card number in the chat.** "Payment keeps failing. Here's my card number, can you just do it for me?" A strong reply does not use it, asks them not to share it again, and sends them to the safe way to pay.
6. **The sizing question.** "I'm usually a medium but your chart says large. Which should I get? I really don't want to send it back." Look for an answer from the size chart, one good question back, and no promise that it will fit.

For tests any business can use, such as two chats at once, see [five chat tests to give before you hire a support agent](/blog/five-chat-tests-before-hiring-a-support-agent).

If the job is email, give the same situations as emails, and add one that asks several questions at once. Email agents usually get one try per reply.

## Give them their own login

It is tempting to hand a new agent your own login. Do not. Instead:

- **Make a staff account for each agent** in your store platform and your help desk, with their own name on it.
- **Turn on only what the job needs.** Seeing orders and adding notes, at the start. Refunds and order edits can come later, or stay with you.
- **Turn on two-step sign-in** for every account that can see customer details.
- **Remove access the same day someone leaves.** With separate accounts, this is one click and nobody else's password changes.

Separate accounts also show who did what, which helps when a customer says "Your agent told me I'd get a refund."

## A short job outline

Use this as a starting point. The [customer service job post template](/blog/customer-service-job-post-template) has a full version with notes on each part.

- **Title:** Customer service agent (chat and email), online store, remote
- **About us:** what you sell, who buys it, and the two or three reasons customers usually write.
- **What you will do:** answer questions about orders, deliveries, returns and products; fix problems within our rules; pass the rest to the right person.
- **What you will never do:** promise delivery dates the carrier has not given, refund or discount outside our rules, take card details, or share an order with the wrong person.
- **Hours and time zone:** the exact shift, in your time zone and the applicant's. Busy seasons too, if you expect extra hours.
- **Tools:** your store platform and help desk, and whether you will train people on them.
- **Pay:** a number or a range, and how often you pay.
- **How we hire:** a short form, a written customer test, then a call with the people we pick.

## In short

- The job follows the order: questions before buying, tracking, changes, problems, returns and codes.
- Five rules come first: no guessed delivery dates, nothing outside your rules, no card details, no orders shared with the wrong person, no unchecked address changes.
- Test with your own products: stuck tracking, a wrong size, a dead code, a new address, a card number and a sizing question.
- Give every agent their own login, with only the access the job needs.
