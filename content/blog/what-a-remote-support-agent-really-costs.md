## Your time to hire

Hiring takes time even when the role is simple:

- writing the job post;
- reading applications;
- testing the people who look good;
- calls with the best few;
- the first week of training.

If you read every application yourself, the hiring time can cost more than the first month of pay. Testing everyone the same way first, and reading only the strongest, is how you keep it small.

## Tools

Count every tool the agent needs a seat in: the help desk or shared inbox, the chat tool, a phone line or calling app for phone work, and any internal tools. Some charge per agent per month.

## Training and the first month

A new agent is slower in the first weeks. Plan for:

- written answers to the questions customers ask most;
- someone on your side to answer the agent's questions;
- reviewing their first chats or calls and giving feedback.

## The cost of a wrong hire

The most expensive agent is the one who leaves in a month, or who upsets customers before you notice. You pay to hire twice, train twice, and repair some customer relationships. A short work sample before the hire (a real customer chat, a few situations from your own business) costs far less than one wrong hire.
