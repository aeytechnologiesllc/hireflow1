The short answer, from a real hire: in November 2026, a US online business run by the people behind HireFlow hired chat support agents in the Philippines at **$350 a month** for four days a week on one fixed eight-hour shift. The first month, which is training, is paid at **$250**. The team lead on the same team is paid **$500 a month** for a full-time, five-day week.

Those are real figures for one business, not a market survey. Pay differs by skill, shift, hours and channel. But the pay is only part of the cost. Here is everything to budget for, so the number you plan with is the number you spend.

## The pay itself

Agree these in writing before the first day:

- **The amount and the currency.** The figures above are in US dollars, paid monthly.
- **What it covers.** Hours a week, days a week, and the shift, written in both time zones.
- **The first month.** If the first weeks are training, say whether they are paid, and at what rate. In the hire above, the first month was paid at a lower rate: two weeks of training, then two weeks of real chats with feedback.
- **Overtime, holidays and night shifts.** What happens when someone covers a teammate's shift, and which holidays they take. Philippine holidays will not always match yours.
- **How you will pay.** The method, the day of the month, and who pays any transfer fees.

When you set the amount, look at what similar jobs on the board you post on offer for the same hours and the same skills. A shift that falls at night in the Philippines, or a job that needs strong written English, is worth more. So is a lead who coaches others.

## Your time to hire

Hiring takes time even when the role is simple:

- writing the job post;
- reading applications;
- testing the people who look good;
- calls with the best few;
- the first weeks of training.

A clear post brings many applicants. The chat agent job above drew more than a hundred in its first day. If you read every application yourself, your hiring time can cost more than the first month of pay. Testing everyone the same way first, and reading only the strongest, is how you keep it small. There is a method for that in [screening 150 applicants without reading 150 résumés](/blog/screening-150-applicants-without-reading-150-resumes).

## Tools

Count every tool the agent needs a seat in: the help desk or shared inbox, the chat tool, and any internal tools or admin panels. Some charge per agent per month, and a seat for a new agent in training costs the same as a seat for your best one.

Ask about the agent's own setup too. A remote chat agent needs a steady internet connection and a computer that can run your tools. Some businesses also ask for a backup connection or backup power where outages are common. Check it before the hire, not on the first day.

## Training and the first month

A new agent is slower in the first weeks, and that is normal. Plan for:

- written answers to the questions customers ask most;
- a clear list of your rules, above all the rules on money and accounts;
- someone on your side to answer the agent's questions during their shift;
- reading their first chats with them, and giving feedback early and kindly.

A paid first month that mixes training with real chats, as in the hire above, gives you time to see real work before you commit, and gives the agent a fair start.

## The cost of a wrong hire

The most expensive agent is the one who leaves in a month, or who gives customers wrong answers before you notice. You pay to hire twice, train twice, and repair some customer relationships.

The cheapest protection is a short work sample before the hire: a live chat with a customer from your kind of business, including one hard moment, such as a customer who is upset about money. A good chat test shows how someone checks before they answer, and what they do when they are not sure. Those two habits matter more than speed. There are examples in [five chat tests to give before hiring a support agent](/blog/five-chat-tests-before-hiring-a-support-agent).

## In short

- Pay: from one real hire, $350 a month for four days a week after a paid training month, and $500 a month for a full-time team lead. Agree the amount, the currency, the hours and the first month in writing.
- Your time: keep it small by testing everyone the same way and reading only the best few.
- Tools: count every seat, and check the agent's own setup.
- Training: plan the first month, and pay for it.
- Avoid the wrong hire: test real work before you hire.
