[OnlineJobs.ph](https://www.onlinejobs.ph) is one of the biggest places to hire remote workers in the Philippines, and support jobs do well there. That is the good news and the problem at once. A clear support post can bring in more than a hundred applicants in its first day. The people behind HireFlow have posted chat support jobs there twice: the first drew 155 applicants, and the second passed 100 in its first day.

This guide covers the whole path: writing the post, handling the flood, testing everyone the same way, and making the hire.

## Before you post

Decide four things first. They go in the post, and they decide who applies.

- **The channel.** Chat, email, or both. Say it in the title.
- **The shift,** in Philippine time and in yours. The Philippines is UTC+8 all year and never changes its clocks, so when your clocks change, the Philippine hours move by an hour. Say so in the post.
- **The pay,** with the currency and how often you pay. Applicants on the board compare pay across posts. Leave it out, and the first question in your inbox will be about pay.
- **What a good first month looks like.** Training, then real work with feedback. If the first month is paid at a different rate, say so.

Check what each plan on the board lets you do. You can post on the free plan, but messaging applicants needs a paid plan. Look at the current plans on the site before you start, so you are not stuck the moment the first good applicant writes in.

## Writing the post

Applicants scroll through many posts. Yours should answer their questions before they ask them.

1. **A title they search for.** "Customer Service Representative (Chat), Remote, Full Time" is found more often than a title only your company uses.
2. **Your business in two sentences.** What you do, and who your customers are.
3. **What the agent will do,** in a short list: the questions they will answer, the tools they will use, and what they pass on to someone else.
4. **The shift and the days,** in both time zones. If the shift is at night in the Philippines, say so plainly. Some people want night work, and the ones who cannot do it will not apply.
5. **The pay** and the first month.
6. **What they need:** their own computer, a steady internet connection, a quiet place to work, and a plan for power or internet cuts.
7. **How to apply,** and how long it takes.

Leave out any age preference. Lines such as "below 30" or "fresh graduates only" can break Philippine law, and so can asking for a birth date while people apply. There is more on that in [what you can't ask Filipino applicants](/blog/what-you-cannot-ask-filipino-applicants). This is general information, not legal advice.

If you want a head start, the [customer service job post template](/blog/customer-service-job-post-template) has a post you can copy and fill in.

## One link for everyone

On OnlineJobs.ph, applicants usually apply by sending you a message. When a hundred messages arrive in a day, each one different in length, order and quality, they are almost impossible to compare.

So give everyone one link to apply through, written plainly in the post: "To apply, complete our application here." Every applicant then takes the same steps in the same order, and you get one list instead of a hundred threads. Reply to the messages that do come in with the same link.

## Test before you read

Most applications look alike on paper. Many cover letters are now written with help from AI, and a polished letter tells you little about how someone handles an upset customer at the end of a long shift. There is more on judging very polished answers fairly in [spotting fake or AI-written applications](/blog/spotting-fake-or-ai-written-applications).

What does tell you is a short work sample, the same for everyone:

- **A check of their computer and connection,** because a remote agent with a weak connection cannot do the job.
- **A short skills check:** a few real situations from your business, timed if the job is live chat.
- **A live chat practice** with a customer from your kind of business, including one hard moment, such as someone upset about a refund.
- **A few written questions** about how they work.

In those hiring runs, the live chat told the hiring team more than any other step. It showed who checks before they answer and who asks when they are not sure, and those two habits mattered more than speed. There are examples you can use in [five chat tests to give before hiring a support agent](/blog/five-chat-tests-before-hiring-a-support-agent).

## Reading the results

Score every answer the same four ways:

1. Did they solve the problem?
2. Was the tone right for your customers?
3. Was the English clear?
4. Did they follow your rules, above all on money and accounts?

Then read only the strongest few closely, and call the best three. A short video call is enough. The work sample has already shown how they work, so use the call for what writing cannot show, and for their own questions about the shift, the pay and the start.

## Answer everyone

Applicants on the board apply to many jobs. Someone who spent an hour on your application deserves an answer, yes or no. Say in the post that everyone who finishes every step will hear back, and keep to it. A short, kind no is enough.

## After the hire

- **Agree the pay, the currency and the payment method in writing** before the first day.
- **Ask for ID after the hire,** not while people apply. That is also when you collect the date of birth.
- **Plan the first month:** written answers to your most common questions, someone on your side to answer theirs, and feedback on their first chats.

## In short

- Decide the channel, the shift, the pay and the first month before you post.
- Write the shift in Philippine time and yours, and say if it is at night.
- Never ask for a birth date, or for an age in years or a bracket, while people apply.
- Give everyone one link and the same short test, with a live chat at its center.
- Score every answer the same way, read the top few and call the best three.
- Answer everyone who finished.
